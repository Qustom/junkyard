# One-Way Commitment
**Category:** Extraction mechanic depth

## The mechanic
Crossing a marked threshold into a deep zone **seals the early exit behind you**: the doorway you came through clangs shut and the entry gate is no longer a viable retreat. There is no toll, no decay — the door is simply *gone*. The deep loot ahead is now do-or-die: you either find a *forward* extract on the far side or you die in the zone. The tension is pure and binary — you stand at the lip and decide *before* committing, with full information, because once you cross the choice is unmakeable. It is the sharpest possible version of "one more room?": the room costs you the road home.

## What exists today
Bands are graded by `DepthGrader` (B3): every `PlacedPiece` carries `depth_index` (BFS hops from entry) and `dist_to_gate` (reverse BFS home). On the linear M1 spine these are equal, so **you can always freely backtrack to the entry gate** — there is no seal anywhere. Extraction is the `extract_gate` (E1/E2) plus the E2 push/cash-out decision; `ReturnCost` (R2) already *taxes* retreat past a `r2_depth_threshold` (clock/exposure/meter tolls), but a tax is reversible — you can always pay it. Nothing makes return *impossible*.

The machinery to seal an exit already exists. `SocketSealer` caps floor-cells-facing-void with WALL tiles at materialisation; the lever/door verb (player-mechanics e2) supplies a triggerable one-way door. What's missing: a **threshold piece** that, when the player crosses it deep-ward, converts the just-traversed link into a sealed wall (or a one-way door), and a `dist_to_gate` recompute so the HUD/extract logic knows the old gate is unreachable.

## How to fit it in
Mark one piece at `depth_index == commit_threshold` as a **commit gate**. On deep-ward crossing, reuse `SocketSealer._place_wall_cap()` on the link cells (or drop a one-way door from the e2 verb), then re-run `DepthGrader.compute_return_distance()` so `dist_to_gate` repoints to the *forward* extract — which a forward gate must now exist (pairs naturally with procgen e3 verticality one-way flow and d4 asymmetric entry/exit, where the band already drains forward). This sharpens E2 (no cash-out-and-leave hedge past the commit) and dovetails with R2 (the seal is the limit case of "return cost → ∞"). New `RunConfig` knob `r?_one_way_commit` (off = today's free backtrack baseline) + telemetry: `commits_made`, `post_commit_death_rate`, `post_commit_extract_rate`, time-to-decision at the threshold.

## Research (cited)
The pattern is textbook **precommitment / bridge-burning**: eliminating your own retreat option to force forward play, which raises payoff on success and lowers it on failure ([Effectiviology](https://effectiviology.com/precommitment/), [Game Theory 101](https://gametheory101.com/courses/game-theory-101/tying-hands-burning-bridges/)) — "an advancing army can precommit by burning the bridges behind them." Roguelite prior art: Spelunky's one-way doors and locked descents, Dead Cells' one-way drops, Hades' chamber commits, and FTL's no-return jumps all stage irreversible forward steps that trade safety for reward, and rely on **carried player knowledge** to make the commit fair across runs ([Roguelike — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roguelike)). The design lesson from Hades' "frictionless" framing ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-with-i-hades-i-creative-director-greg-kasavin)): a hard commit must be *telegraphed and chosen*, never sprung.

## Open questions
- **Fairness / death-trap.** If the forward extract is unreachable (blocked, no resources to fight through), the seal is an unfair kill. Does the generator *guarantee* a forward extract beyond every commit gate, or is the commit only offered when one is provably reachable? — needs Director review.
- **Telegraphing.** How loud is the warning? A distinct piece silhouette + a "this seals behind you" prompt + a beat to retreat — vs. a subtle threshold that punishes inattention. Tone call. — needs Director review.
- **Beacon bypass (s3).** Can an emergency-beacon / panic-extract item override a seal, or is the whole point that nothing does? A bypass softens the tension to near-zero; no bypass risks feeling cheap. Recommend: beacon extracts you *out* (ends the run) but never re-opens the entry — preserves do-or-die while giving one paid escape hatch. — needs Director review.
- **Interaction with R2 toll.** If both ship, does the toll apply *up to* the commit and the seal *past* it (clean handoff), or are they mutually exclusive configs? — design call.
