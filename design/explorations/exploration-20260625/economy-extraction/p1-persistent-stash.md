# Persistent Stash / Vault
**Category:** Run-to-run persistence

## The mechanic
A **safe store** — money and/or items — that lives in **meta-state**, separate from the disposable run bag and from the per-run quota cash. It is the home for value you have *secured*: it survives a death/timeout (subject to the reset-severity dial, p4), and it is where long-term goals live — saving for the expensive gear tier (s1), hoarding a buffer against a thin run, stockpiling rare junk for a future recipe. Above the per-run grind ("clear this quota or die"), the stash is the slow strategic layer: a number (and a shelf of goods) that only goes up when you choose to bank, and the visible scaffold for "what am I working toward over 20 runs."

## What exists today
**A money-only stash already exists.** `game_state.gd:33` `var money: int` is meta-state, serialized via `to_meta_dict()` (`:547`), never reset between runs — only a quota *miss* (`wipe_meta()`, `:410`) zeroes it. Banked junk *identities* also already persist: `banked_junk: Array[JunkItem]` (`:42`) carries un-sold items across runs, persisted **by id** through the `JunkCatalog` under the objects-OFF save model (`to_meta_dict():551`, rehydrated `_rehydrate_banked_junk():584`). So the run→meta transfer at the gate (`extract_and_end_run():226`) and on a loss (`fail_run()` pockets, `:459`) is **built** — value crosses the boundary correctly today.

What's *missing* is a **deliberate vault as a place**, distinct from "the sell-queue." `banked_junk` is a transient holding pen — F2's `sell_banked_junk()` (`:324`) **empties it to Money on the next sell screen**. There is no storage the player *chooses to keep items in* across many runs (a Tarkov-style shelf), no capacity, no "stash vs. sell-now" decision. A real vault adds: (a) an item store the player deposits into and withdraws from at will (not auto-flushed at sell), and (b) optionally a money sub-balance walled off from quota churn. Save implications are modest because the persistence spine exists: a vault item-array serializes by id exactly like `banked_junk` — but it is a **new meta field**, so it costs a `schema_version` bump + a stepwise migration (default empty array for old saves), per `save_manager.gd` discipline.

## How to fit it in
- **Vault = new meta array**, sibling to `banked_junk`: `var vault_junk: Array[JunkItem]` plus optional `var vault_money: int`. Serialize ids in `to_meta_dict()`, rehydrate via the catalog — the proven pattern. Withdraw moves an item back into the *next* run's bag (run-state) at the yard, before `start_run`.
- **It is the home for q2 rollover and s1's funds.** Under q2 *rollover* (the current default), surplus money simply is the stash. Under q2 *expire/deduct*, the vault becomes **load-bearing**: cash churns each cycle, but goods you stashed survive — banking items, not money, is the only cross-cycle carry. s1's expensive tiers are "save the vault up to $500."
- **Reset-severity dial (p4) governs vulnerability.** The key tuning question: *is the vault safe on a loss?* Options span p4's dial — fully safe (Hades-style: meta untouchable, the vault is the soft-landing); partially raided (lose a % on death, Tarkov "lose your raid loot but keep the stash" sits here); or fully wiped on a quota miss (today's `wipe_meta()` already zeroes everything — the harshest pole). Pick the vault's safety **with** p4, not separately.
- **Save discipline:** bump `schema_version`, add a migration step defaulting `vault_*` to empty/0, add a QA save-migration fixture (CLAUDE.md save rule).
- **RunConfig knob + telemetry:** `vault_enabled` (off = M1.4 baseline, no vault field touched) and `vault_loss_on_fail` (0..1, feeds p4). Telemetry: stash **size over time** (item count + value per run-end), deposit/withdraw events, and "value parked in vault vs. spent into sinks" — the gate signal for *does the vault give long-term goals, or just let players hoard and coast?*

## Research (cited)
**Escape from Tarkov** is the archetype: a persistent grid stash (10×28 Standard up to 10×72) that survives raids while the *run* loot is what's at risk — and crucially, **seasonal/wipe characters start fresh**, so "a stacked permanent stash won't help the new profile." Two lessons: stash *space* is itself a meta-sink (containers like the Junk Box multiply effective storage — capacity is a thing you upgrade), and the stash's *vulnerability* is a deliberate dial (raid-loss vs. wipe-safe). **Diablo's shared stash** is the gentler pole — a safe, cross-character vault with no loss, purely an organization/hoarding convenience. **Hades' House Contractor** shows the *goal-scaffold* role: persistent gems/diamonds spent on permanent unlocks give "long-term goals across runs" — meta-progression's defining feature vs. pure roguelikes. THE FAR YARD already sits on the persistent side (money is meta); a vault formalizes the *item* half of that.

## Open questions
- **Item-vault vs money-only (needs Director review).** A money-only stash is *free* — it already exists. A real item vault is the meatier feature (hoard rare junk, stash-vs-sell decisions, supports q2-expire) but costs UI, a save field, and inventory-management friction. **Recommendation:** ship money-only as the baseline (already true), and prototype the item vault *only if* q2 lands on expire/deduct (where it becomes load-bearing) or s1 wants rare-junk crafting inputs. Don't build the vault UI speculatively.
- **Stash capacity as a sink?** Tarkov makes *space* an upgrade. Capacity-limited vault → "buy more shelves" is a clean Salvage/Yard-track sink and forces stash-vs-sell tension; unlimited vault is frictionless hoarding. Director call on whether we want the management layer.
- **Vulnerability on loss → defer to p4.** Whether the vault survives a death (safe), bleeds a % (raided), or is wiped on a quota miss (today's behaviour) is *the* p4 reset-severity decision. Resolve jointly, not here.
- **Save schema cost.** Each new meta field = a `schema_version` bump + migration + QA fixture. Cheap individually, but every persistence exploration (q2 vault-money, s5 debt, this) adds one — batch the schema additions into one migration when several land together.
