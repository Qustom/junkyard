# Grid / City Blocks
**Category:** Maze & density archetypes

## The archetype
A band laid out as a **regular street grid**: rectangular building blocks separated by straight, clean streets that run the full length and width of the level. The defining read is the **long, unbroken sightline** — stand at a street corner and you can see all the way down the avenue. Streets are the *exposed* space (fast travel, but you can be seen and shot from far off); building interiors are the *safe* space (loot, cover, dead-ground from any street lane). Spatially it's the most **legible** archetype in the maze group: you always know which way is "down the street," intersections are obvious commit points, and corners are obvious safety. That legibility makes it the natural **teaching** floorplan — it instructs the player, without a tutorial, that *streets are dangerous, corners and doorways are how you survive ranged threats.*

## How it fits THE FAR YARD bands
The grid is a clean staging of the extraction loop's core tension. **Streets = ranged danger + speed**; **interiors = loot + breather.** Pushing down a street is fast travel toward depth but maximally exposed; ducking into a building is safe but costs dive-clock time (`dive_clock_changed`) and a detour off the through-line. So every intersection is a literal "fast-and-exposed vs. safe-and-slow" micro-decision, repeated block by block — the macro "push deeper or extract now?" call rendered at street scale. Richer junk sits *inside* buildings (`JunkPlacer` already depth-scales `base_sell_value` and gates tier by `curve.min_tier`), so leaving the street to loot is the deliberate trade.

This is a strong **early / teaching band — Band 1 (Near)**, and it fits the surface fiction cleanly: the first portal-deep junkyard reads as a half-buried *town* of stacked wrecks and container-blocks — orderly because it's the shallowest, least-warped layer. Deeper bands can *decay* the grid (jittered, broken, overgrown) so legibility itself becomes a depth signal.

Hosting the opposition spread: long streets are the canonical **lane-control** stage. The **Sentry** (`2-sentry.md`) watching one avenue, the **Suppressor**, the **Lobber** arcing over a block — all want exactly these clean firing columns, and the player's intended counter is the building **corner**: break line-of-sight by stepping behind a block, then cross the lane on the cooldown gap. That is the whole "corners counter ranged" lesson the grid teaches. For the **throw** verb, a street is a perfect mouse-aimed throw lane — a long clear column to a Sentry at the far end — while interiors give the close-quarters fallback.

## Generation approach (on the real bandgen system)
A regular grid is a natural fit for the socket-snapping generator because pieces already mate on an **integer cell lattice** (`_alignment_offset` abuts footprints flush; `band.fits()` rejects overlap). Two routes, in rising effort:

- **Catalog-and-weights (cheapest, no algorithm change):** author B1 **building pieces** as square multi-socket floor rects whose sockets sit on the *block edge facing a street*, plus **street-segment** and **intersection (4-socket cross)** pieces that are thin walkable corridors. The existing `_generate_once` frontier growth then naturally tiles building→street→intersection→street→building. `socket_sealer.gd` walls unused sockets so a block's street-facing wall stays solid except at its **doorway socket**. This already runs deterministically — every draw is on the `RNG` autoload at the same site/order, so `test_bandgen_determinism.gd` holds unchanged.
- **Lattice-snap pass (truer grid, modest extension):** to guarantee *aligned* avenues rather than an organically wandering spine, add a placement constraint that snaps building origins to a `block_pitch` lattice (building span + street width) — a pure integer rounding of `_alignment_offset`'s result, so it stays deterministic and byte-reproducible. Streets then line up across the whole band instead of staggering.

**Depth + loot:** `DepthGrader.grade()` measures BFS hops, which down a grid tracks "blocks from the gate" — deeper blocks read as farther in *and* farther home (`dist_to_gate`). `JunkPlacer.plan()` layers loot into building interiors (its weighting keys off `floor_cells`, which are the room interiors, not the streets), and `loot_density_per_area` (J3, ships off) would reward bigger blocks proportionally. Streets get little/no junk by virtue of being thin low-floor-cell pieces — reinforcing "value is *off* the exposed lane."

## Flavor knobs
- **Block size** — small blocks = many intersections, twitchy/chaotic; large blocks = long commits, fewer but tenser crossings.
- **Street width** — narrow (2-cell) = claustrophobic, easy to throw across; wide = true long-range sightlines, deadlier for Sentry/Suppressor lanes.
- **Building-interior count** (`target_piece_count` share that is buildings vs. streets) = total loot rooms and dive-clock exposure.
- **Doorway frequency** — sockets per building street-face: one door = forced single entry (tense, predictable); multiple = fluid weaving, weaker chokepoints.
- **Grid regularity** — *perfect* lattice (snap pass on) reads as clean teaching city; *jittered* (snap off, or a small seeded offset on `block_pitch`) breaks monotony and signals deeper, more-warped bands.

## Synergies & tensions
- **Ranged oppositions:** the headline synergy — Sentry, Suppressor, Lobber, Patroller-cone all thrive on the clean street column, and the corner/doorway is the built-in counter, so the floorplan *teaches its own opposition*. Tension: **melee-rush** actors (Charger, Pack hunters) are weak here — they get shredded crossing exposed streets and need the open-field archetype instead. A band should pick a register.
- **Dive clock:** streets are fast (good — efficient travel), but the safe route hugs corners and ducks buildings, which is *slower*, so the grid converts time-pressure into a continuous speed-vs-safety dial. Wide streets + ranged Sentries make the safe route much longer — strong tension, easy to over-tune into tedium.
- **Depth grading:** clean fit on a linear/branched grid; near-perfect "deeper = farther + richer." If the lattice-snap pass creates **loops** (a true city has them — multiple routes between two blocks), `DepthGrader`'s structural BFS already handles loops correctly, and `loop_back_count` (stubbed) is the future hook for deliberate shortcut streets back toward the gate — an extraction-relevant tactical layer.
- **Combining archetypes:** the grid is a great **macro skeleton** with another archetype *inside* a block (a single building interior could be an open-floorplan or maze fill) — the socket model makes a "block" just a piece. Also composes with **Field** hazards confined to one street (gas cloud filling an avenue via `room_bounds`) for a "this lane is closed" reroute puzzle.

## Open questions
- **Avoiding the monotony of a perfect grid.** A flawless lattice is legible but can read as sterile/repetitive across a whole band. *Recommendation:* ship the snap-pass with a **seeded jitter knob** (small per-block offset + occasional missing/merged block) so regularity is a *tunable depth signal*, not a fixed look — perfect at Band 1, decaying deeper. **Effort + fun call — surface to the Director after a layout playtest; it may also be art/palette's job more than the floorplan's.**
- **Does the grid need true loops, or is a linear/branched street-spine enough?** Loops sell "city" and add extraction route choice, but require the stubbed `loop_back_count` work and risk muddying the clean depth read. *Recommendation:* prototype linear-grid first (zero new algorithm), add loops only if playtest finds the single-route grid too on-rails. **Scope call — Director.**
- **How wide is a street before the sightline is unfair vs. just tense?** Pure feel, coupled to player move speed, Sentry bolt speed, and throw range — not resolvable on paper. *Recommendation:* prototype 2–3 street widths and let the M-gate fun playtest decide. **Fun call — Director.**
- **Does "city blocks" undercut the junkyard fantasy by reading as a generic town?** Like discrete-rooms, the floorplan is conventional; the junkyard identity must come from piece art (blocks = stacked wrecks/containers, not houses), palette, and `depth_grader`/theming — not the grid itself. **Vision/tone call — Director:** is the teaching-legibility win worth the familiarity cost for the player's *first* band?
