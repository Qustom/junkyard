# Discrete Rooms + Connectors
**Category:** Room-and-corridor archetypes

## The archetype
Distinct chambers joined by explicit hallways — the classic dungeon read. The eye parses a band as a *sequence of bounded encounters* with hard thresholds between them: you are *in* a room (a place where things happen) or *in* a corridor (a place you traverse). Corridors are the seams; doorways are the commit points. Spatial feel: legible, punctuated, deliberate — the opposite of an open cave's "where does this even end." Each room is a self-contained risk/reward unit with a clear inside and a clear exit. This is exactly what the as-built generator already produces with `branch_chance == 0.0`: a linear spine of room → corridor → room, sealed by `socket_sealer.gd`.

## How it fits THE FAR YARD bands
The extraction loop runs on a recurring **"push deeper or extract now?"** decision, and discrete rooms make that decision *legible*: each chamber is a unit the player can appraise (loot visible, threats visible, exits visible) before committing across the threshold. A corridor between rooms is a natural **commit beat** — once you step in, you've decided; the dive clock keeps ticking and `DepthGrader.dist_to_gate` grows, so every doorway crossed is a longer walk back to the gate. That tension — deeper rooms hold richer loot (`JunkPlacer` scales `base_sell_value` by `depth_norm` and gates tier by `curve.min_tier`) but the extraction walk lengthens — is the whole game, and discrete rooms stage it cleanly.

Hosting the explored opposition spread: rooms are ideal **Actor arenas** (Charger needs a sightline lane, Pack hunters need open floor, Sentry/Spinner need standoff space) and the bounded chamber gives the `room_bounds` that **Field** hazards flood-fill into (gas cloud, electrified floor — the gas sketch already reuses L2's `room_bounds` confinement). Corridors are the natural home for **Fixtures** and chokepoint pressure: pop-up spikes, crusher pistons, sweeping lasers, the ping-pong hazard pacing endpoint-to-endpoint down a long hall. The `rooms_only` / J3 corridor-gate flag in the opposition descriptor maps directly onto this floorplan — dense Actors in rooms, sparse trap-Fixtures in connectors. For the **core verbs**: bounded rooms give clean **mouse-aimed-throw sightlines** (a wall is a wall, no ambiguous half-cover); corridors create the tense single-file traversal where a thrown item is your only reach; loot clusters legibly per-room; and the entry piece (always `catalog[0]`, kept safe by the spawn-exclusion radius) is the extraction anchor.

## Generation approach (on the real bandgen system)
This archetype is the generator's *current default*, so "producing it" is mostly authoring the right `piece_catalog.gd` and tuning `bandgen_config.gd`:

- **Pieces** (`zone_piece.gd`, B1 format): author two clear *kinds* — **rooms** (large multi-socket floor rects) and **corridors** (narrow long-hall pieces; `RunConfig.CORRIDOR_PIECE_IDS` already names a 16-cell long hall). A discrete look comes from making rooms visibly chambered (4+ floor-cell width) and corridors visibly narrow (2-cell `width_cells` socket lanes).
- **Algorithm** (`BandGenerator._generate_once`): unchanged — entry at origin, grow the frontier socket-by-socket. `_weighted_pick_index` draws a compatible piece; `_alignment_offset` mates it flush; `band.fits()` rejects overlaps. To *enforce* the rooms-via-corridors rhythm rather than hoping the weights produce it, the cleanest extension is a **alternation constraint** in `_try_attach_piece`: bias/require a corridor after a room and a room after a corridor (a small "last-kind" check against the host piece's id-class). That keeps rooms from bleeding directly into each other — the defining feature of the archetype.
- **Connectors / sockets:** corridors are just pieces with two opposite sockets (N/S or E/W); `socket_sealer.gd` walls off every unused socket so dead-end rooms read as proper chambers, not open wounds. Branching (`branch_chance > 0` via R4) turns the linear spine into a tree of rooms — still discrete, now with dead-end reward pockets.
- **Depth + loot:** `DepthGrader.grade()` annotates each piece by BFS hops; `JunkPlacer.plan()` then layers loot per-room scaled by `depth_norm`, with `loot_density_per_area` (J3, ships off) able to reward bigger chambers proportionally — a natural fit since rooms here are *meant* to be the loot units.
- **Determinism:** every draw stays on the `RNG` autoload at the same site/order; the alternation check is pure integer/id comparison, so `test_bandgen_determinism.gd`'s seed-reproducibility holds. Junk rides its own `_JUNK_SALT` sub-stream, untouched.

## Flavor knobs
- **Corridor length** (author 1-cell vs. 16-cell hall pieces; `lvl_short_corridors` drops the long one): long = tense single-file traversal + clean Fixture lanes; short = rooms bleed together (less "discrete," faster pace).
- **`target_piece_count`** (`bandgen_config`): band length / total encounter count = total dive-clock exposure.
- **`branch_chance`** (R4): 0.0 = pure spine (one path, every room mandatory); >0 = branching tree with optional dead-end reward rooms (more "explore vs. extract" choice).
- **Branching factor** = how many sockets rooms expose (author 3-4-socket rooms vs. 2-socket): wider = more forks per room.
- **Dead-end frequency:** governed by branch chance × leftover sockets the sealer caps; dead-ends = high-risk loot pockets off the spine.
- **Corridor rarity** (`lvl_corridor_weight_mult`, J4): down-weighting corridors compresses the layout toward room-adjacency (blurs the archetype); up-weighting stretches it (more traversal, more tension).
- **Room size variance** (piece catalog authoring): mixing small + large rooms creates pacing rhythm and feeds `loot_density_per_area`.

## Synergies & tensions
- **Oppositions:** strong synergy with sightline/standoff Actors (Charger, Sentry, Patroller cone) and bounded Fields (gas, electrified) that want `room_bounds`; corridors host Fixtures and the ping-pong pacer. Tension with **map-scale time-pressure** Fields (Rising tide, Spreading fire) — those want continuous flooded space, and hard room/corridor thresholds with sealed walls fight a rising water level (a sealed wall stops the flood unphysically). Tethered-pair also chafes: the tether line wants open room, not a corridor that bisects it.
- **Dive clock:** corridors *are* clock pressure made spatial — every connector is dead time you pay in exposure, which is good design tension but, overused (long corridors + high count), becomes tedious traversal. Balance via `target_piece_count` × corridor length.
- **Depth grading:** near-perfect fit — `depth_index` increments cleanly per room, `dist_to_gate` tracks the literal backtrack walk, so "deeper = richer + farther home" reads exactly as the player feels it.
- **Combining archetypes:** yes — discrete rooms can be the *macro* skeleton with another archetype as a room's *interior* (e.g. a single large room is internally an open-cave or maze fill). The socket model makes a "room" just a piece, so a piece could itself be procedurally sub-generated. Also composes with loop-closing (`loop_back_count`, currently stubbed) to add shortcuts back toward the gate — an extraction-relevant tactical option.

## Open questions
- **Alternation enforcement: hard constraint or weights only?** A hard "no room touches a room" rule guarantees the archetype but can starve generation (more `band.fits` rejections → more whole-band retries, risking the soft floor). Weights-only is robust but occasionally produces room-bleed. *Recommendation:* soft bias first (boost the opposite-kind weight after each placement), measure retry rates, escalate to a hard rule only if the look drifts. **Effort/fun call — surface to Director after a layout playtest.**
- **How long is a "tense" corridor before it's just tedious?** Pure feel; depends on player move speed and dive-clock rate. *Recommendation:* prototype 2–3 corridor lengths and let the M-gate playtest decide — **a fun call for the human Director, not resolvable on paper.**
- **Dead-ends: punish or reward?** In an extraction game a dead-end is a gamble (extra exposure for off-spine loot). Worth it only if `JunkPlacer` weights dead-end pieces richer than spine rooms — which needs a "is_deadend" piece annotation the generator doesn't currently compute. **Scope call:** add the annotation, or accept dead-ends as flavor-only for now?
- **Does this archetype risk reading as "generic dungeon" and undercutting the junkyard fantasy?** The discrete-room look is strong but conventional; the junkyard identity has to come from `depth_grader`/`junk_placer` theming, piece art, and band palette, not the floorplan. **Vision/tone call for the Director** — flag if the legibility win is worth the familiarity cost, or whether a messier archetype better sells "every junkyard, fused."
