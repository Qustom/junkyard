# Oppositions (Hazards) — Cross-Exploration Analysis
**Set:** hazards/  ·  **Analyzed against:** procgen-bands, player-mechanics, economy-extraction, hub-staging
**Date:** 2026-06-25 · Authored by `game-director-designer` (cross-exploration fan-out). Not yet dispositioned by the Director.

> The oppositions set is the **"what you face"** half of a loop whose other three halves live elsewhere: the **verbs** that beat oppositions (`player-mechanics/`), the **spaces** that host them (`procgen-bands/`), and the **stakes** that make facing them matter (`economy-extraction/`, `hub-staging/`). Almost every hazard doc already names a verb, a band, or a clock it leans on — this analysis makes those edges explicit, finds the double-ups, and surfaces the shared substrates none of the individual docs could see alone.

---

## Synergies (this set reinforces / is enabled by / pairs with)

### A. Oppositions ⇄ the spaces that host them (procgen-bands)
The band README states it outright: *"the bands are the spaces where verbs and oppositions meet."* The opposition architecture's Actor/Field/Fixture taxonomy (`hazards/0-scalable-opposition-system.md`) maps almost one-to-one onto the band archetypes' floorplans.

1. **Ranged group (`hazards/2-sentry`, `2-lobber`, `2-spinner`, `2-suppressor`) ⇄ Open field with cover (`procgen-bands/b1-open-field-with-cover`).** This is the strongest single cross-link in the set, and *both sides already name it*: b1 says long sightlines are "exactly where the mouse-aimed throw and the ranged opposition spread come alive… the natural host for the ranged group," and every `2-*` doc assumes open standoff space (a Sentry's lane, a Spinner's spiral, a Lobber's arc all need sightline). They are co-dependent: ranged enemies are trivial in corridors, and an open field is empty without something that punishes exposure.
2. **Ambusher (`hazards/1-ambusher`) + Burrower (`hazards/1-burrower`) ⇄ Organic caverns (`procgen-bands/b3-organic-caverns`).** b3 names both by name: low sightlines are "the natural host for the low-sightline opposition spread — the Ambusher (disguised in a nook beside a juicy pickup) and the Burrower (denying a blobby chamber on a timer) both *need* poor sightlines to read as fair-but-tense rather than trivial." The cavern's ragged fringe *is* the Ambusher's hiding mechanic.
3. **Pursuers (`hazards/1-charger`, `1-pack-hunters`, `1-leaper`) ⇄ Discrete rooms + connectors (`procgen-bands/a1-discrete-rooms-connectors`).** a1 calls rooms "ideal Actor arenas (Charger needs a sightline lane, Pack hunters need open floor, Sentry/Spinner need standoff space)" and corridors "the natural home for Fixtures and chokepoint pressure." The Charger's locked-vector lane wants a room's clear floor; the Pack's flank wants open space the player can deny with a corner/doorway.
4. **Field hazards (`hazards/4-gas-cloud`, `4-electrified-floor`, `4-magnet-field`) ⇄ bounded `room_bounds` (`procgen-bands/a1`).** Every Field doc reuses the L2 `room_bounds` Rect2 as its flood/zone container, and a1 confirms "the bounded chamber gives the `room_bounds` that Field hazards flood-fill into (the gas sketch already reuses L2's `room_bounds` confinement)." Discrete rooms are the cleanest host for Fields because the walls define the zone edge.
5. **Static traps (`hazards/3-popup-spikes`, `3-crusher-piston`, `3-sweeping-laser`) ⇄ corridors / the `rooms_only` gate.** The opposition descriptor's `rooms_only` / J3 corridor-gate flag (`hazards/0-`) maps directly onto a1's floorplan: "dense Actors in rooms, sparse trap-Fixtures in connectors." A crusher *is* a corridor; a Sentry lane *is* a chokepoint.
6. **Mirror (`hazards/1-mirror`) + Reflector (`hazards/6-reflector`) + Spinner (`hazards/2-spinner`) ⇄ Band 3 "Lateral / physics-off" fiction.** All three docs self-assign to Band 3, and the band set's E-flavor "verticality fakes / wear-decay" plus the GDD's "reality instability" give them a fictional home. The Mirror in particular wants `procgen-bands/e1-symmetry` (a point-mirror in a symmetric room is always heading for you — geometry sets difficulty).

### B. Oppositions ⇄ the verbs that beat them (player-mechanics)
The player README states the relationship: *"the verbs multiply against the oppositions."* Each hazard category was deliberately built to exercise a specific verb.

7. **The whole throw-synergy group (`hazards/6-eater`, `6-reflector`, `6-armored-shelled`, `6-thief`, `6-mimic-loot`, `6-weight-plate`) ⇄ the throw verb set (`player-mechanics/t1`–`t6`, L1/L6).** This is the set's deepest verb-coupling. The `6-*` group exists *to decompose the throw into a decision space*: Eater = "should I throw at all?", Reflector = "throw, but where?", Armored = "throw *which* item?", Mimic = "throw to *scout*", Weight-plate = "throw to *traverse*". Reflector explicitly pairs with **bounce-throw (`player-mechanics/t4-bounce-wall-throw`)**: t4 says "the Reflector becomes a true duel — it bounces *your* throw back, so your own bounce knowledge predicts the return path," and both use `Vector2.bounce` (a deterministic, solvable duel — flagged as a possible over-solvability risk in t4).
8. **Pursuers (`hazards/1-charger`, `1-leaper`, `1-pack-hunters`, `5-the-hunter`) ⇄ Dash/dodge (`player-mechanics/m1-dash-dodge`).** m1 names the charger/leaper/pack as its primary test cases and frames "THE FORK" (i-frames vs pure-repositioning) explicitly around them: pure-reposition counters pursuers via spacing; i-frames additionally beat the Leaper's *lunge frame* and let you dash *through* a sweeping laser or popup-spike. The fork's measurable signature *is* whether dash trivializes the opposition spread.
9. **Patroller vision-cone (`hazards/1-patroller-vision-cone`) ⇄ Sneak (`player-mechanics/m3-sneak-crouch`) + Hide (`player-mechanics/e3-hide`).** This is a hard mutual dependency, not just a synergy: m3 and e3 both state they are *hard-blocked on the patroller landing first* ("until at least one of those ships, sneak reduces an input nobody reads"; "hide has nothing to hide from"). The patroller is the *only* opposition that gives the stealth verbs something to do — they ship together or not at all.
10. **Sound-aggro zone (`hazards/4-sound-aggro-zone`) ⇄ Noise→aggro substrate (`player-mechanics/x2-noise-aggro`).** x2 names the sound zone as "the canonical listener — its `noise` accumulator becomes 'sum of `noise_emitted` inside the zone.'" The zone is the *consumer* of a substrate the player set must build; throw-as-lure (the zone's headline synergy) is x2's throw-burst event. See Shared Dependencies.
11. **Darkness pocket (`hazards/4-darkness-pocket`) ⇄ Use/consume light (`player-mechanics/u1-use-consume`) + Deploy light (`player-mechanics/u2-deploy-place`).** u1 lists REVEAL/LIGHT effects as "the player-side counter to the `4-darkness-pocket`"; u2's deployed light "suppresses the darkness overlay." The darkness pocket is the *demand* that makes the light verbs worth carrying — a clean problem/answer pair that ships without an HP pool.
12. **All Field/zone hazards (`hazards/4-*`, `5-rising-tide`, `5-spreading-fire`) ⇄ Deploy/place (`player-mechanics/u2`).** u2 is *built on the opposition architecture* — it spawns the same `bomb_hazard`/`hazard_entity` nodes with an `owner` flag flipped to target enemies. The player becomes a hazard-author: deploy a trap to bait pack-hunters, a decoy to pull a pursuer. u2 explicitly "reuses the scalable-opposition prefab roster from `0-scalable-opposition-system.md`."
13. **Thief (`hazards/6-thief`) + Weight-plate (`hazards/6-weight-plate`) ⇄ the slot inventory + deliberate-drop (`player-mechanics/i3`).** Both hazards read inventory state (`run_haul_value()` / `used_slots()`) as their trigger — the Thief steals from it, the Weight-plate fires on it. Weight-plate's counter ("throw your haul across, walk light, regrab") is a fusion of throw + i3's drop; the Thief that *lightens* your bag could even let you cross a plate you couldn't before (an emergent cross-hazard combo i3 enables).

### C. Oppositions ⇄ the stakes (economy-extraction, hub-staging)
The economy README: *"the economy is why any of it matters once you surface."* The time-pressure group is where oppositions and the economy fuse.

14. **Alarm-spawner (`hazards/5-alarm-spawner`) ⇄ Greed-escalation (`economy-extraction/r2-greed-escalation`).** r2 is the *reward-side mirror* of the alarm's threat-side: it explicitly "reuses the alarm-spawner's room-dwell seam wholesale — the same `dwell_t` accumulating inside `room_bounds` drives **both** the spawn threshold and a new value ramp." Together they form one complete per-room push/cash-out microdecision (milk the room for rising value vs. flee the rising waves). r2 flags they may *be* the same feature.
15. **Carry-load enemies (`hazards/5-the-hunter`, `5-rising-tide`, all pursuers) ⇄ Carry-load→speed (`player-mechanics/x1`) + Full-bag liability (`economy-extraction/r3`).** x1/r3 make a heavy bag slow; the docs note the load curve must dip player top-speed *below* the pursuer's chase speed so "I can't outrun it carrying this" lands. The Hunter (a band-wide chaser that wakes late, exactly when the bag is heaviest) is the perfect partner: it weaponizes the greedy late-dive bag. r3 names the full-value-bag-near-a-draining-clock as the synthesis moment.
16. **Spreading-fire (`hazards/5-spreading-fire`) ⇄ the loot economy directly.** Unlike every other hazard (which threatens *you*), spreading-fire threatens *the haul* — it destroys loot value before you can grab it, making B3 depth-scaled value "literally perishable." It is the most economically-coupled opposition: it sharpens the push-vs-extract math by shrinking the prize in real time.
17. **The Hunter / Alarm-spawner / Rising-tide ⇄ Timed-arming extraction (`economy-extraction/e2-timed-arming`).** e2 turns the gate into a channeled final encounter and explicitly wants the pursuers: "this is where pursuers earn their keep — the channel is a magnet that *invites* the fight. Pair it with L2/the Hunter so the last seconds are a stand." The time-pressure group supplies the interrupt threat e2's channel needs.
18. **Mimic-loot (`hazards/6-mimic-loot`) + Ambusher (`hazards/1-ambusher`) ⇄ Search-containers (`player-mechanics/e1`).** e1's timed-search is a *vulnerability window* and names the synergy: "Pair with `6-mimic-loot` — a container that *is* a mimic triggers at the end of its search; with ambushers that wake while you're rooted." The Mimic-as-container is e1's cruelest variant. Both punish the looting moment the economy rewards.
19. **Map intel (`economy-extraction/s4`, `hub-staging/c3`) ⇄ the whole opposition deck.** s4's "hazard forecast" tier sells a manifest of *what opposes you this run* — a pre-computed peek at which `OppositionDef` cards the band Director will deploy. This makes the opposition spawn-deck (`hazards/0-`) a thing the economy can *sell information about*. The richer the deck, the more intel has to describe.

## Contradictions & tensions (conflicts, double-ups, competition)

1. **The HP-pool dependency splits the Field/zone group off into M2 — and it's a *shared* M2 gate.** `hazards/0-` flags it, and the master README confirms it is "a recurring M2 prerequisite." **Gas cloud (`hazards/4-gas-cloud`)**, **electrified-floor (`hazards/4-electrified-floor`)**, **spreading-fire and rising-tide as *damage* (`hazards/5-*`)**, plus **heal items (`player-mechanics/u1`)** and any damage-taking tradeoff ALL block on the same player health pool that M1 lacks (M1 is binary instant-death). The tension: several of the most distinctive "gradient, survivable" hazards — the ones that would most differentiate the build from "everything is instant-death" — are the ones gated furthest out. Either fake them with a per-second clock-drain in M1 (gas's OQ proposes this) or accept the Field group is a coherent M2 wave.

2. **The clock-doubling cluster: three+ oppositions all lean on the same ~300s dive clock as their pressure, risking redundancy.** The hazard set's category-5 README question is literally *"does it double up with the clock?"* — and the answer varies dangerously:
   - **The Hunter (`hazards/5-the-hunter`)** *IS* the countdown dramatized (it reuses `dive_clock` as its wake trigger). Its own OQ admits "two things saying 'time's up'" — feature or redundancy is the load-bearing Director call.
   - **Greed-escalation (`economy-extraction/r2`)** + **Alarm-spawner (`hazards/5-alarm-spawner`)** are a per-room dwell timer; r2 admits it risks "collapsing into 'the whole dive gets richer + harder,' which the clock + depth curve already do" and asks whether r2 and the alarm "should be one feature."
   - Stacking **Rising-tide + Spreading-fire + Alarm in one room** is flagged by multiple docs as "probably too much together; pick one per room archetype."
   The stakes: the build could end up with four mechanics all expressing "you stayed too long," diluting each. Needs a deliberate **division of pressure scales**: global-dive (clock/Hunter) vs. per-room (alarm/greed) vs. per-loot (fire) vs. per-space (tide) — *one per scale*, not four redundant ones.

3. **Dash i-frames (`player-mechanics/m1`) vs. the throw as the signature threat-answer — and the throw-synergy group's whole premise.** The `6-*` group is built on the assumption that *the throw is the answer* (Eater punishes throwing, Reflector demands aimed throwing, etc.). If dash ships with i-frames (the m1 fork), many threats become "dodge it, don't kill it," which m1 itself warns "hollows out the throw's signature role." This isn't just a movement tuning call — i-frames would *undercut the entire premise of the inventory/throw-synergy opposition category*. m1's recommendation (ship pure-repositioning default, A/B i-frames) protects the `6-*` group; the Director should weigh them together, not separately.

4. **The Reflector + player bounce-throw = a fully deterministic, possibly *solved* duel.** Both `hazards/6-reflector` and `player-mechanics/t4-bounce-wall-throw` use `Vector2.bounce`. t4 flags it: "if both the player throw and the Reflector use `Vector2.bounce`, the duel is deterministic — fun mastery, or too solvable?" Two docs independently reaching for the same reflection primitive is elegant, but a deterministic reflection war may flatten into a memorized line rather than a read. Needs a playtest read on whether determinism here is *mastery* or *triviality*.

5. **Stealth oppositions need an enemy-perception substrate that doesn't exist — and it's the gate for a whole pillar.** The patroller vision-cone (`hazards/1-patroller-vision-cone`), sound-aggro zone (`hazards/4-sound-aggro-zone`), sneak (`player-mechanics/m3`), and hide (`player-mechanics/e3`) all depend on **enemy perception (vision cones + a hearing/noise model)** that the build entirely lacks. The contradiction: the GDD names stealth as a *first-class pillar*, but it is the single most under-built substrate across all five sets, and four explorations are stranded behind it. Either commit to building the perception substrate as a named prerequisite wave, or scope stealth down to "a light option" (m3's own recommendation) and accept the patroller/sound-zone as the only stealth content.

6. **Map-scale time-pressure Fields (`hazards/5-rising-tide`, `5-spreading-fire`) fight the discrete-room floorplan.** a1 names this directly: "Tension with map-scale time-pressure Fields (Rising tide, Spreading fire) — those want continuous flooded space, and hard room/corridor thresholds with sealed walls fight a rising water level (a sealed wall stops the flood unphysically)." The socket-sealer's walls (which make discrete rooms legible) are exactly what a room-spanning flood can't cross believably. These Fields want the *open* archetypes (b1/b3), not the room-graph default.

7. **Top-down flat map vs. hazards that assume elevation or Z.** Rising-tide ("flooding low-first needs a faked per-tile height field… or it just looks like an expanding stain"), the Burrower/Leaper (wall-ignoring needs a Z-state), and arc-over-wall throws (`player-mechanics/t2`) all want a height model the engine doesn't have. The hazard docs lean on "disable `world` collision mid-state" as the cheap fake — workable, but a cluster of hazards quietly assuming verticality on a flat map is a latent feel risk.

8. **Tethered-pair (`hazards/1-tethered-pair`) and Mimic-loot (`hazards/6-mimic-loot`) don't fit the opposition architecture cleanly.** `hazards/0-` calls them out honestly: Tethered-pair's threat is "a relation between two bodies — no component model wants to own a constraint between hosts" (wants a dedicated coordinator); Mimic-loot "must masquerade as a `JunkItem` and spawn through `JunkPlacer`, straddling the loot and opposition systems." These two are the architecture's genuine edge cases — not reasons to fork it, but flagged special cases that cost more than "def + component."

9. **Non-lethal oppositions break the `*_kills`-toggle house style.** The Suppressor (`hazards/2-suppressor`) "deliberately has no `*_kills` knob — that *is* its identity," and conveyor/ice/magnet Fields are non-lethal force modifiers. The M1.5 contract assumes every opposition routes through `fail_run` behind a `*_kills` toggle; a growing class of non-lethal oppositions (suppressor, conveyor, ice, magnet, sound-zone, darkness) needs the architecture to treat "lethality: none" as first-class (which `hazards/0-`'s `lethality` enum already does — but the RunConfig/telemetry conventions assume a kill channel).

## Shared dependencies & build-order notes

Cross-system prerequisites this set both **needs** and **provides**:

- **The opposition architecture (`hazards/0-scalable-opposition-system`) is the spawn host that three other sets touch.** The player's **deploy/place (`player-mechanics/u2`)** spawns its prefabs with an `owner` flag; the band **Director (`procgen-bands/0-`)** hands it placement context (the two architecture docs "share a clean data seam — a generated band hands placement context to the opposition spawner"); and **map-intel (`economy-extraction/s4`)** reads its spawn-deck to sell forecasts. `hazards/0-` is foundational infrastructure, not just a hazard refactor — build-order it *before* u2 and s4's hazard tier.

- **The enemy-perception substrate (vision cones + the noise/hearing model) is a prerequisite this set *provides the demand for* but the player set *specifies*.** `player-mechanics/x2-noise-aggro` defines the `EventBus.noise_emitted(pos, loudness, source)` contract and the `HearingTrigger` component; `hazards/0-` already lists `SightlineTrigger` and `ProximityTrigger` as components and notes a `HearingTrigger` "is the same shape." **Build order:** the EventBus noise contract + `HearingTrigger` first (a named substrate task), then the patroller vision-cone, then sneak/hide/sound-zone land as small additions. This substrate gates: `hazards/1-patroller-vision-cone`, `hazards/4-sound-aggro-zone`, `player-mechanics/m2-sprint-cost` (noise fork), `m3-sneak`, `e3-hide`.

- **The player HP pool is the shared M2 gate** for the Field/zone damage hazards (`hazards/4-gas-cloud`, `4-electrified-floor`, the damage variants of `5-rising-tide`/`5-spreading-fire`) AND `player-mechanics/u1` heal items AND any damage-taking tradeoff. One decision (HP pool: M2) unlocks a coherent wave across two sets. Until then: force-only Fields (conveyor, magnet, ice, sound-zone, darkness — all non-HP) can ship in M1.x; chip-damage Fields wait.

- **The throw-vs-static-hazard hit rule is a shared prerequisite** for several "answerable trap" ideas. `hazards/3-turret-on-track`, `3-popup-spikes` (OQ: kills hazards?), `3-flame-vent` (OQ: damages enemies?), and `hazards/0-`'s `ThrowInteraction` component all want a throw/projectile to *hit a Fixture/Field*, which the L1 throw currently only does against body-based Actors. Whoever builds the first destructible Fixture defines this rule for all of them.

- **The `room_bounds` Rect2 primitive (from L2) is the shared container** that every Field hazard (`hazards/4-*`, `5-*`) and the alarm/greed dwell-timer (`hazards/5-alarm-spawner`, `economy-extraction/r2`) reuse. It already ships. Build-order-free, but worth noting it's the single most-reused primitive across the time-pressure + zone groups.

- **Seed-pinning (provided by `economy-extraction/s4`/`hub-staging/c3`) lets the opposition deck be forecast.** The opposition Director's card draws are deterministic from `(seed, config)` (`hazards/0-`); s4 needs the next-run seed pinned as meta-state to sell a hazard forecast. The determinism the opposition architecture already guarantees is what makes the intel product *truthful by construction*.

## Notable design information

- **This set is the "what you face" half of a four-part loop, and it is unusually well-cross-referenced already.** Nearly every hazard doc names a verb, a band, and a clock it leans on (the `2-*` ranged docs name b1; the `6-*` throw docs name t4 and L1; the `5-*` docs name the dive clock and r2/r3). The edges are mostly *intended* — the contradiction work is the value-add, not the synergy-finding.

- **Two opposition categories were built as *anti-cheese counter-lessons* and depend on the rest of the build having taught the cheese first.** The Splitter (`hazards/1-splitter`: "killing is the bad default"), the Eater (`hazards/6-eater`: "don't throw here"), and the Burrower (`hazards/1-burrower`: "the throw is useless most of the time") only land *after* L1 has trained "throw to kill everything." Their first-appearance bands (2–3) are correct — but this means they are explicitly **second-order content** that the M1.x build can't validate until the throw loop is internalized. They're not standalone graybox tests.

- **Mismatch in assumed scope/milestone across the time-pressure group.** The Hunter, Alarm-spawner, and (force-only) Rising-tide can ship in M1.x against existing systems; the *damage* variants and the chip-Fields need M2 HP. The category reads as one group but splits cleanly into an M1.x-shippable half (timer-triggered chasers/spawners reusing existing hazards) and an M2 half (gradient/attrition). Worth tagging per-doc so a wave doesn't try to ship gas alongside the Hunter.

- **Opportunity the individual docs missed: the non-lethal "force modifier" Fields (conveyor, ice, magnet) are the cheapest, earliest-shippable, highest-leverage cross-multipliers in the entire set.** They need no HP pool, no perception, no `*_kills` channel — they just modify movement/throw vectors. And they make *every other hazard deeper* (conveyor-into-spikes, ice-before-laser, magnet-into-pit) without adding a lethal entity. `hazards/0-` already recommends shipping force-only Fields sooner. They also directly enrich the throw verb (lead-the-shot across a conveyor, curve-the-throw in a magnet field) — the signature verb the whole `6-*` group is built around. This is the under-celebrated quick win.

- **The player-as-hazard-author (`player-mechanics/u2-deploy-place`) inverts the whole set.** Deploy means the player spawns the *same* opposition prefabs (bomb, decoy, light) for their own ends. This is a rare two-way edge: the opposition system isn't just "what the player faces," it's *also* "what the player wields." Designing oppositions with an `owner` flag in mind (per u2) keeps that door open cheaply.

## Top 5 things for the Director

1. **Resolve the clock-doubling cluster by assigning one pressure per scale.** The Hunter (`hazards/5-the-hunter`), Alarm-spawner + Greed-escalation (`hazards/5-alarm-spawner` ⇄ `economy-extraction/r2`), Rising-tide, and Spreading-fire all express "you stayed too long." Pick: global-dive = clock/Hunter, per-room = one of {alarm, greed} (likely merged), per-loot = fire, per-space = tide — *not* all stacked. **Highest-value contradiction to resolve.**
2. **Decide the dash fork (`player-mechanics/m1`) *with* the throw-synergy `6-*` group in view, not separately** — i-frames would hollow out the entire "throw is the answer" premise those six oppositions are built on. Recommend pure-repositioning default, A/B i-frames (m1's own rec).
3. **Greenlight the non-lethal force-Fields (conveyor, ice, magnet) as an early M1.x wave** — they need no HP/perception/kill-channel, ship against existing movement seams, multiply every other hazard's depth, and enrich the signature throw. The cheapest high-leverage cross-win in the set.
4. **Treat enemy perception (vision cone + noise/hearing) as one named prerequisite substrate** — it gates four explorations (`hazards/1-patroller-vision-cone`, `4-sound-aggro-zone`, `player-mechanics/m3`, `e3`) and the GDD's stealth pillar. Build the EventBus noise contract + `HearingTrigger` + patroller cone as a unit, or scope stealth to "a light option."
5. **Ratify the HP-pool-at-M2 line and tag the Field/zone group accordingly** — it cleanly splits the time-pressure and zone categories into an M1.x-shippable half (force-only Fields, timer-triggered chasers) and an M2 half (gas, electrified, chip-damage), and simultaneously unblocks `player-mechanics/u1` heal. One decision, two sets unblocked.
