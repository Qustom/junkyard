# Search Containers
**Category:** Interaction & environment verbs

## The mechanic
A **container** (chest, locker, rusted safe, glovebox) that does not drop its loot the instant you press interact. Instead, holding interact runs a **search timer** — a second or three with a progress ring filling — and only on completion does the loot spawn/enter your bag. This stands in deliberate contrast to today's **instant floor pickup**: a `JunkPickup` is grabbed in a single frame on a single press. Containers concentrate more/better loot than scattered floor junk, but they **charge you in seconds against the dive clock**. The verb is no longer "see loot → take loot"; it's "commit to standing here, exposed, for N seconds — is it worth it right now?" That commit decision is the whole point: it converts looting from a free reward reflex into a wager paid in the only currency the dive truly rations — time.

## What exists today
Honest read: **all loot is instant floor pickup.** `entities/junk_pickup/junk_pickup.gd` is an `Area2D` carrying an `Interactable` child (the A2 composition). The player's `InteractionDetector` finds the nearest `Interactable` and, on the `interact` *press edge* (`_unhandled_input` → `event.is_action_pressed("interact")`), emits `EventBus.interaction_requested(id, target)`. `JunkPickup._on_interaction_requested` immediately calls `RunInventory.try_add(item)`, fires `junk_picked_up`, and `queue_free()`s on accept. There is **no hold-to-interact path, no timer, no progress UI** — `interact` is purely a discrete press. The interaction layer also has no concept of "this interactable takes time" or "this interaction can be interrupted." The dive clock (`systems/dive_clock.gd`) drains in `_process` and exposes the guarded `modify_light(amount)` mutator (already used by the R3 `exposure_clock_tax` seam) — so a search has two ways to "cost time": just let the clock keep draining while you stand still, or actively `modify_light(-search_cost)` on completion. What's missing for timed containers: a `Container` entity (sibling of `JunkPickup`), a **hold detection** path in the detector (press → start, release/leave → cancel, elapsed → fire), a progress-ring UI, an **interrupt rule**, and the EventBus signals to broadcast search start/cancel/complete.

## How to fit it in
**Dive clock tie-in (the thesis):** the simplest, truest cost is *the clock keeps draining while you search* — no special subtraction, the seconds you spend are just seconds you don't have for the next room. Containers therefore make the clock's value legible: a 3s search visibly eats your light. Optionally add an explicit `modify_light(-search_cost)` flat tax for a sharper feel; recommend the **passive-drain-only** version first (it reuses existing systems and reads honestly). **Placement/density:** route containers through `systems/depth/junk_placer.gd` / `junk_spawner.gd` as a distinct plan-entry type — fewer containers than floor junk, each holding a richer payload, so the deeper you go the more "search vs. flee" tension compounds with the clock. **Oppositions (the reason timed search is interesting):** the search window is a **vulnerability window**. Pair with `hazards/6-mimic-loot.md` — a container that *is* a mimic triggers at the end of (or partway through) its search; with ambushers that wake while you're rooted; with an **alarm spawner** that ticks during the search. **Interrupt-on-hit:** taking damage (or an enemy entering melee) cancels the search, refunding nothing but the loot — the seconds are spent. **Control mapping:** reuse `interact`, but the detector must distinguish *hold* from *press*: start on press over a "timed" interactable, advance while held + still focused, cancel on release / focus-loss / damage event, complete on elapsed. Twin-stick controller uses the same hold on the interact button. **RunConfig knob + telemetry:** add a `cont_`-grouped block to `data/run_config/run_config.gd` mirroring the `r1_`/`r3_` pattern — `cont_enabled: bool = false` (all-off default = today's instant-only world), `cont_search_seconds`, `cont_interrupt_on_hit`, `cont_clock_tax`. New EventBus signals: `container_search_started(target)`, `container_search_cancelled(target, elapsed)`, `container_search_completed(target)`. Telemetry logs search count, total/average search time, **interrupted-search rate**, and loot-per-search vs. loot-per-floor-pickup — the numbers the fun gate needs to ask "is searching worth it?"

## Research (cited)
Prior art for **timed looting under threat** is well-trodden. *Escape from Tarkov* makes container search a multi-second, audible, screen-locking act, and the community's blunt lesson — "looting in exposed areas for too long" gets you killed because "containers make noise, bodies attract attention, and open spaces punish greed" — is exactly the vulnerability-while-looting tension this mechanic imports ([Tarkov Looting wiki](https://escapefromtarkov.fandom.com/wiki/Looting), [loot guide](https://boostroom.com/blog/escape-from-tarkov-loot-guide-most-valuable-items-to-pick-up)). Tarkov also surfaces *already-searched* containers as a visual state, signalling spent loot to others — a readable-world idea worth borrowing for solo too (don't re-offer an emptied chest). *Lethal Company* expresses the same "looting makes you vulnerable" axiom through **two-handed scrap**: grabbing the most valuable items disables climbing, picking up more, and other actions ("HANDS FULL"), creating a commit-cost on greed without an explicit timer ([Two-handed items wiki](https://lethal.miraheze.org/wiki/Category:Two_handed_items), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/598512477964147999/)). *Hunt: Showdown* and *DayZ* similarly tie looting to a rooted, interruptible animation window. The shared design truth across all of them: **the time/commitment cost is what makes a loot decision a decision** — instant pickup has no decision in it.

## Graybox sketch
Smallest version: a `Container` scene = `Area2D` (re-skin of `JunkPickup`'s greybox as a box) + an `Interactable` child with `prompt_text = "Search"`. The detector, on press over it, starts a timer and shows a **progress ring** (a `_draw`-arc on the prompt, filling 0→1 over `cont_search_seconds`). Release or walk away → ring resets, no loot. Ring full → spawn the floor-pickup(s) it held (or `try_add` directly) and emit `container_search_completed`. No interrupt yet — just hold-to-complete vs. the clock draining behind you. That alone is playtestable: one chest, one ring, the clock ticking.

## Open questions
- **Interrupt rules.** Does taking damage cancel (lose the seconds, keep no loot) or pause (resume on safety)? Cancel is harsher and more readable; pause is forgiving. Recommend **cancel-on-hit** for the tension, dialed by `cont_interrupt_on_hit`. *Director fun/tone call.*
- **Cost model: passive-drain-only vs. explicit clock tax.** Reuse the existing drain (search = standing still = light lost) or also `modify_light(-cost)` for a sharper bite? Recommend passive-only first; it's honest and adds no new system. *Recommend, validate at the fun gate.*
- **Search-time vs. reward scaling.** Flat seconds per container, or longer search = richer/tiered loot (a "deep search" you can bail early on)? Tiered is richer but more UI. Recommend flat for graybox. *Defer.*
- **Mimic integration timing.** If a container is a mimic (`6-mimic-loot.md`), does it trigger at search *completion* (you committed, you pay) or *mid-search* (the ring itself is the tell — it stutters)? Mid-search is crueler and more interesting; recommend completion-trigger first for clarity. *Director call.*
- **Re-search / empty state.** Show an opened-but-empty container (Tarkov-style) so the player doesn't re-pay for nothing — yes, recommend a spent visual. *Low-risk, recommend.*
