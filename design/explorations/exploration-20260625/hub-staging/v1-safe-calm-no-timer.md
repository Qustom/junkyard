# A Safe, Calm, No-Timer Space
**Category:** The hub as a pressure release / pacing valve

## The idea
A small, warm, walkable room you return to between dives where **nothing is draining**. No `DiveClock` counting down your light, no `ExposureMeter` ticking up, no entities, no push/cash-out decision — just you, free movement, and a soft ambient bed. The hub is defined less by what it *adds* than by what it *removes*: the held breath of the dive (the clock is a literal "held breath" per `dive_clock.gd`) is finally let out.

That exhale is the reward. The cheapest, highest-leverage reason a hub exists is **contrast manufacturing**: a peaceful beat between scares resets the player's tolerance so the *next* dive's clock and dread land harder, instead of desensitising into noise. You don't buy anything, you don't progress a meter — the absence of the meter is the prize. It is also the GDD's tone pillar made literal: "the yard unsettles; the town heals... Neither half works without the other" (Pillar 3). The hub is the smallest possible unit of "the town heals."

## What exists today
Honest read: there is **no calm beat anywhere in the loop**. The current shape is `menu → dive (clock + exposure pressure) → sell → repeat` — every state is either active pressure or a flat menu. The dive holds the breath; nothing un-holds it. `dive_clock.gd` and `systems/oppositions/exposure_meter.gd` are the tension instruments; `audio_director.gd` already escalates intensity by band depth (`current_intensity = depth`) — but it has **no calm bed to escalate *away from*** and resolve *back to* (its `_on_player_died` TODO even says "resolve to surface/overworld bed" — that bed is the hub). The contrast the GDD demands is currently one-sided: all yard, no town. There is **no hub scene** yet.

## How it could fit in
A greybox warm room: one `ColorRect`/tiled floor in surface golden-hour palette (GDD §visual contrast), free `CharacterBody2D` movement reusing the dive player, **no clock node instantiated and no exposure ticking** — the run lifecycle signals (`run_started`/`run_ended`) simply aren't emitted here, so the clock literally cannot exist. `audio_director.gd` plays a soft, low-intensity cue (`current_intensity = 0`) — the calm pole the band-escalation curve resolves toward. The player wanders, the music is quiet, then they choose a portal and the breath re-tightens; the dive's first clock-tick hits *because* the room had none. Cheap to graybox (a room, a floor, an ambient loop — days, not weeks). This is the **baseline hub: likely always-on**, not `RunConfig`-gated — it's the control surface other hub features (v2 rent-pressure, vendors, NPCs) bolt onto, so it should be the permanent default in `run_config.gd`, with experiments layered *on top* of the calm baseline.

## Research (cited)
Prior art converges hard on tension-via-contrast. Resident Evil's **safe rooms** are the canonical case: a psychologist notes players find them *relaxing precisely by contrast* with the horror outside, and the music deliberately mixes calm piano with anxious strings so safety reads as "temporary" — the rest exists to make the next scare hit harder and prevent desensitisation ([gamingbible](https://www.gamingbible.com/news/psychologist-reveals-why-resident-evil-save-rooms-relaxing-774113-20260302), [Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/features/resident-evil-save-rooms-kept-us-safe-from-the-stresses-of-survival-horror/), [PekoeBlaze](https://pekoeblaze.wordpress.com/2021/07/05/safe-rooms-in-horror-games/)). **Hades' House of Hades** uses the return-home beat as a calm, story-bearing pause between runs that makes defeat feel meaningful rather than punishing ([Polydin](https://polydin.com/hades-game-design/), [Roguelite House](https://roguelitehouse.com/articles/hades.html)). **Firelink Shrine** is the largest safe zone in Dark Souls — crackling-fire warmth that is "eerie yet comforting," explicitly emphasising "the tension between safety and the dangerous world beyond" ([Fextralife](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Firelink+Shrine), [Fandom](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Firelink_Shrine)). All three: the calm is not filler, it is the instrument that gives the danger its edge.

## Open questions
- **How calm before it's boring?** A truly inert room risks dead air. The RE answer is "calm with a thread of unease" (warm piano + tense strings). Does the Far Yard hub want *pure* relief, or a faint undertow (a distant surf sound per GDD §3, a Cyrus tape) that keeps it from going flat? **Tone call — Director.**
- **Can the player dawdle forever?** With no clock, nothing stops infinite loitering. Is that fine (pure relief, trust the player to get bored and dive) or does it sap urgency from the meta-loop? This is the seam where **v2 rent/debt-pressure** could undercut the relief — if the surface itself nags, the exhale stops being an exhale. Recommend: keep v1 hub *genuinely* timer-free and put any pressure on a slow *day-level* clock (sleep advances the day), never an in-hub meter.
- **Does the calm need to *do* anything?** v1's thesis is "no" — absence is the reward. But playtesters may read an empty room as unfinished. **Fun call — Director**, best answered at the M1-style fun gate: graybox the bare room first, measure whether the contrast reads before adding vendors/NPCs.
