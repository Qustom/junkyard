THE FAR YARD — CHANGELOG
========================

M1.11 — "The Far Field"   (changes since M1.10)
-----------------------------------------------

Until now every dive led to a place with walls close around you — rooms and corridors, or
a winding cave. M1.11 adds a FOURTH dive band that is the opposite: one wide-open field
where you can see (and be seen) clear across the whole space. It is built by a THIRD kind
of level generator, and it comes with two new dangers that only make sense out in the open
— threats that reach you AT RANGE instead of chasing you down. The first three dives are
completely unchanged (walk into any of the three old gates and they play exactly as
M1.10). The new band is a deeper, more exposed place where standing still gets you killed.


A FOURTH DIVE — "THE FAR FIELD" (new)
-------------------------------------
* NEW — A fourth dive portal on the hub. Opposite the cave-teal Warren gate there's now a
  fourth gate with an indigo glow, prompt "Dive — The Far Field". Walk into it to dive into
  the new open-field band. The three original gates still dive into band 1, The Sump, and
  The Warren exactly as before — all four now sit around the yard so you choose where to go.
* NEW — The Far Field: an open field, not a building or a cave. Where band 1 and The Sump
  are assembled from rooms and corridors and The Warren is a winding cave, The Far Field is
  carved by a NEW open-field generator — one vast flat arena with cover scattered sparsely
  across it (hugging the edges), and long sightlines everywhere. It plays completely
  differently: instead of reading doorways or poking cave nooks, the whole game becomes
  "where do I stand, and what can see me." A full-width clear lane runs across it — a fast,
  exposed highway — while the loot sits out on the open floor, tempting you off cover. A
  cold-indigo "a wrong, too-open expanse" tint sets it apart. It sits a step deeper than The
  Warren, with a heavier opposition budget to match.


TWO NEW HAZARDS — Far-Field-only, and they hit you FROM RANGE (new)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
* NEW — The Lobber ("The Mortar"). A slow, stand-still sheller. On a rhythm it reads your
  position and drops a ground-circle MARKER where you're standing, then lobs an arcing shell
  onto that spot after a short flight — the marker is your fair window to walk off it. Its
  whole identity: the shell ARCS, so it IGNORES cover — ducking behind a wall does NOT save
  you, only MOVING does. Camp a safe-looking corner and it will rain you out. A thrown item
  kills it and silences the rain.
* NEW — The Sentry. A stationary emplacement that watches one straight LANE, its line always
  drawn on the ground so you can read it. Step into the lane and it flashes (a windup you can
  react to), then fires a fast bolt straight down the lane — but a wall or a piece of cover
  STOPS the bolt cold. This is the band's cover dialogue: the Sentry is why cover keeps you
  safe, the Lobber is why you can't just sit behind it. You beat a Sentry three ways: route
  around its lane, cross during its cooldown gap, or throw an item to KILL IT PERMANENTLY —
  spend an item to open that route for good.
* The Wrecker (charger) also returns in The Far Field's deck — its telegraphed straight dash
  reads differently out in the open, with a long run-up and room to dodge — alongside a
  scattering of proximity mines that punctuate the crossing.
* Both new hazards are exclusive to The Far Field in this build — bands 1, The Sump, and The
  Warren stay exactly as they were, so you can feel the difference cleanly.
* The debug Oppositions tab (press P) now tunes these two as well — every hazard, old and
  new, exposes its own sliders there, and The Far Field's natives are marked "IN DECK" and
  open pre-expanded, so you can set values in the hub and dive in to feel them.


NOT YET IN THIS BUILD
---------------------
* The new open-field generator is THE FAR FIELD ONLY — bands 1, The Sump, and The Warren are
  unchanged (still their own generators). The Far Field is the proof that a third, totally
  different kind of level generation slots in cleanly.
* The new hazards are FAR-FIELD-ONLY — they do not appear in the other three bands (a
  deliberate clean A/B for this build; whether they graduate to other bands is a later call).
* The Far Field's floor, walls, and cover are greybox geometry with a tint pass, not a
  bespoke tileset. The loot out in the exposed center is worth the same as loot near cover
  for now — a "risk pays more" value bias is a later addition.
* The Lobber currently drops its shell where you STOOD, not where you're heading — leading
  your movement is a difficulty dial saved for later.
* The hub plaza is now FULL at four dive gates plus the shop — a fifth band will need a
  proper band-select screen rather than another gate in the yard.
* Save format is UNCHANGED (meta v4 / run v1) — which band you pick is per-dive, not saved.
* The loop, hub, shop, economy, quota, and timer are otherwise unchanged from M1.10.

M1.10 — "The Warren"   (changes since M1.9)
-------------------------------------------

Until now the two dive portals both led to bands built the same way — rooms and
corridors snapped together from prefab pieces. M1.10 adds a THIRD dive band that is
grown, not built: a cave carved by a completely different kind of level generator, with
two new kinds of danger that only live down there. The first two dives are completely
unchanged (walk into either of the old gates and they play exactly as M1.9). The new
band is a deeper, more disorienting place where you can't see far and the threats hide
in the ground.


A THIRD DIVE — "THE WARREN" (new)
---------------------------------
* NEW — A third dive portal on the hub. Next to the two existing dive gates there's now
  a third gate with a cave-teal glow, prompt "Dive — The Warren". Walk into it to dive
  into the new cave band. The two original gates still dive into band 1 and The Sump
  exactly as before — all three sit side by side so you choose where to go.
* NEW — The Warren: a cave, not a building. Where band 1 and The Sump are assembled from
  rectangular rooms and corridors, The Warren is carved by a NEW cavern generator —
  organic, cellular-automata caves. The result looks and plays completely differently:
  blobby, rounded chambers instead of square rooms; winding throats instead of straight
  halls; bad sightlines everywhere; and nooks and dead-ends you have to poke into to find
  the loot. A blue-violet "grown, not built" tint sets it apart from The Sump's amber. It
  sits a step deeper than The Sump, with a heavier opposition budget to match.


TWO NEW HAZARDS — Warren-only (new)
-----------------------------------
* NEW — The Ambusher. A predator that hides IN THE FLOOR — near-invisible, just a faint
  smudge where it's buried. It waits. Loot something near it and it ARMS (a tell flashes
  up), then springs in one fast, lethal lunge at where you stood — the tell is your fair
  window to read it and step clear. But it doesn't stop after one hit: the moment it has
  pounced it becomes a STALKER. It vanishes back into the floor — unhittable again, only
  the smudge tracking across the ground to warn you — and hunts you, closing the gap while
  hidden, then springing again, and again. You can only hurt it in the instant it's
  exposed: bait a pounce, then throw junk into it during the recovery. Greed near a hidden
  Ambusher is the trap; ignoring one that's already sprung is the second. In the cave's
  bad sightlines you often don't see the first spring coming at all.
* NEW — The Burrower ("Sinkmaw"). A tunneling area-denier. It travels UNDERGROUND — you
  can't hit it and it can't hit you while it's buried — following you as a decal that
  tracks along the floor (it moves slow enough to outwalk, and it crosses under walls). On
  a rhythm it TELEGRAPHS, locks its target spot, then SURFACES to strike right there and
  holds for a moment before diving back down. The telegraph is the fairness line: the spot
  it will surface is locked when the tell fires, so stepping off it is always safe. While
  it's buried you simply can't kill it — you punish it in the window it's up.
* Both new hazards are exclusive to The Warren in this build — bands 1 and The Sump stay
  exactly as they were, so you can feel the difference cleanly. They're seeded across the
  whole cave, shallow to deep.
* The debug Oppositions tab (press P) now tunes these two as well — every hazard, old and
  new, exposes its own sliders there, and The Warren's natives are marked "IN DECK" and
  open pre-expanded, so you can set values in the hub and dive in to feel them.


NOT YET IN THIS BUILD
---------------------
* The new cavern generator is THE WARREN ONLY — bands 1 and The Sump are unchanged (still
  the prefab-piece generator). The Warren is the proof that a second, totally different
  kind of level generation slots in cleanly.
* The Warren's portal glow is meant to read cave-teal but currently looks a deeper
  cyan-blue over the placeholder portal art — an art retone is pending.
* Cave floor and walls are greybox geometry with a tint pass, not a bespoke cave tileset,
  and there's no new higher-tier loot down there yet (a later addition).
* The new hazards are WARREN-ONLY — they do not appear in bands 1 or The Sump (a deliberate
  clean A/B for this build; whether they graduate to other bands is a later call).
* Save format is UNCHANGED (meta v4 / run v1) — which band you pick is per-dive, not saved.
* The loop, hub, shop, economy, quota, and timer are otherwise unchanged from M1.9.

M1.9 — "The Sump"   (changes since M1.8)
----------------------------------------

Until now there was ONE place to dive — the portal at the back of the yard. M1.9 adds a
SECOND dive band with its own portal, its own look, and two new kinds of danger that live
only down there. The original dive is completely unchanged (walk into the old gate and it
plays exactly as M1.8). The new band is a harder, denser place with hazards that make you
fight and dodge differently.


A SECOND DIVE — "THE SUMP" (new)
--------------------------------
* NEW — A second dive portal on the hub. Next to the original dive gate there's now a
  second gate glowing ember-orange, prompt "Dive — The Sump". Walk into it to dive into a
  different band. The original gate still dives into the same band-1 dive as before — the
  two are side by side so you can choose.
* NEW — The Sump: a band that feels apart. The Sump is branchier and denser than band 1,
  tinted a sepia-amber "flooded decay" wash, with worn/decayed terrain and a large VAULT
  set-piece dropped into the layout. Its opposition budget is ~15% heavier than band 1, so
  it reads as a genuine step down into somewhere worse. (Placeholder art — the palette is a
  tint pass, not a final tileset.)


TWO NEW HAZARDS — Sump-only (new)
---------------------------------
* NEW — The Wrecker (a charger). A hazard that TELEGRAPHS, then commits to a straight,
  lethal DASH down a shown lane, then has to RECOVER. The loop is bait -> dodge the dash ->
  punish it in the recovery window. In The Sump it is dash-INVULNERABLE — a thrown item
  MISSES it while it's dashing (that's intended), so you can't just facetank it; you kill
  it in the recovery window instead. Crashing it into a wall stuns it longer.
* NEW — The Splitter. A lurker: it sits where it spawned until you come close (roughly
  on-screen range), then locks on and follows you for good. Kill it WITH A THROW and it
  reliably splits into 2 smaller children right where it died — anywhere, even at the band
  entrance. The children chase immediately and don't split again. (Kill it any other way
  and it just dies.) It makes throwing at it a choice, not a reflex.
* Both new hazards are exclusive to The Sump in this build — band 1 stays exactly as it was,
  so you can feel the difference cleanly. They're seeded across the WHOLE band, shallow to
  deep, so the pressure doesn't fade as you push further in.


DEBUG / CONFIG MENU — a generated Oppositions tab (new)
-------------------------------------------------------
* NEW — Oppositions tab (press P). The debug menu has a new Oppositions tab that is
  GENERATED from each hazard's own tuning fields — every hazard (old and new) exposes its
  parameters here. The Sump's hazards (Wrecker, Splitter) are marked "IN DECK" and their
  sections open pre-expanded, so their activation radius and speed sliders are right there —
  set values in the hub, then dive into The Sump to feel them. Tune a hazard's params and
  respawn it live to feel the change. A run you tweak this way is flagged as "debug-dirty"
  so it can be filtered out of the honest telemetry later. All tunable knobs total 91 now
  (89 from before + 2 new opposition levers); "Reset" still returns the all-off baseline
  (byte-identical to M1.0–M1.8).


UNDER THE HOOD
--------------
* The whole point of this version: adding a new hazard or a new band is now DATA, not new
  engine code — hazards are "definition + a small reusable part" and bands are "a profile +
  stages". The two new hazards and The Sump were built almost entirely as data files, which
  is what makes future content cheap. (Nothing player-facing here — just why the above
  shipped fast.)


NOT YET IN THIS BUILD
---------------------
* The new hazards are SUMP-ONLY — they do not appear in band 1 (a deliberate clean A/B for
  this build; whether they graduate into band 1 is a later call).
* The Sump's look is a TINT pass on placeholder tiles, not a bespoke tileset — and the hub
  iso props are not yet re-dressed around the second portal (a watch-item for a later art
  pass).
* Save format is UNCHANGED (meta v4 / run v1) — which band you pick is per-dive, not saved.
* The loop, hub, shop, economy, quota, and timer are otherwise unchanged from M1.8.

M1.7 — "Player Embodiment"   (changes since M1.6)
-------------------------------------------------

This version gives the player a BODY. Until now you played as a greybox box with a
little nose pointing where you aim. M1.7 adds a real, hand-drawn animated character —
the flannel/hoodie "surface" look — that idles and walks in all 8 directions and plays
pickup and throw animations. It renders in BOTH the hub and the dive.

>>> IMPORTANT: THE NEW CHARACTER IS OFF BY DEFAULT. <<<
The build still boots to the same greybox box as M1.6 — identical look and feel — so the
character is OPT-IN. To turn it on: press P to open the debug menu, go to the new PLAYER
tab, and tick "Player art (debug)". The player immediately becomes the animated character
in the hub and in dives. Untick it to go back to the greybox.


THE ANIMATED CHARACTER (new — opt-in)
-------------------------------------
* NEW — 8-direction animated player character. With "Player art (debug)" ON (P -> Player
  tab), the player renders as a real character with idle and walk animations in all 8
  directions, following your aim, plus dedicated PICKUP and THROW animations. Throwing
  reads cleanly in every direction. The character sits correctly on the player's body and
  shows up in both the hub and the dive.
* NEW — Player tab in the debug menu. The P-menu has a new PLAYER tab. It holds the
  "Player art (debug)" toggle and live controls for how pickup/throw animations briefly
  root the player while they play: a lock MODE (Clip-driven or Fixed), separate PICKUP and
  THROW lock durations, lock-on-pickup, and animate-on-rejected-pickup. Tune these live if
  the pickup/throw root feels too long or too short (try Fixed mode with shorter durations
  for a snappier feel).


POLISH
------
* FIXED — One-frame spawn "jump". Freshly-spawned and re-dropped junk, thrown items, the
  player and camera at the start of a dive, and hazards as they appear no longer flash at a

M1.6 — "Surface & Staging"   (changes since M1.5)
-------------------------------------------------

This version gives the game a SURFACE. Until now you booted straight into a debug
harness — a Start button bolted onto the config rail, an auto sell-screen as the only
between-runs beat, and the knob menu in your face on the first screen. M1.6 wraps the
existing dive in a real game shell: you boot to a Main Menu, stage between runs in a
walkable Hub, sell your haul and spend money at a Shop, and depart into dives from
there. The dive itself is unchanged from M1.5 (the all-off baseline is still byte-for-
byte identical to M1.0–M1.5).


MAIN MENU (new)
---------------
* NEW — Real main menu. The game now boots to a proper Main Menu instead of a config
  rail: NEW GAME (start a fresh save — if a save already exists, it asks you to confirm
  before wiping it), CONTINUE (resume your saved game; greyed out when there is no save),
  QUIT, and a SETTINGS button (a "coming soon" placeholder for now). The first-run
  telemetry-consent prompt now lives here.


THE HUB (new)
-------------
* NEW — Walkable hub between runs. You now return to a small staging room between dives
  and walk around it, instead of dropping into a sell screen. The hub holds a DEPARTURE
  PORTAL (walk into it to start a dive) and a SHOP terminal. The dive timer does NOT run
  in the hub — the clock is dive-only. When a dive ends (extract, death, or timeout) you
  are automatically returned here, and your quota is checked on arrival.
* NEW — On-screen controls. The hub shows a list of every control input — move, aim,
  throw, cycle item, interact/extract, pause, and the debug menu — so you can learn the
  scheme at a glance before you dive (keyboard/mouse and controller).
* The HUD now shows your current quota under the "Holding:" readout — what you HAVE
  (banked money plus your current haul) versus what you NEED — so you always know whether
  this run is on track to clear the bar.


THE SHOP — sell + buy (replaces the old sell screen)
----------------------------------------------------
* NEW — Shop you walk to. The hub's Shop terminal opens a shop with two sides:
  - SELL: cash in the haul you banked on your last dive — your junk converts to Money.
    (This replaces the old automatic sell screen; you now choose when to sell.)
  - BUY: spend Money on a small catalog of upgrades that you OWN across runs. Owned
    purchases persist through quit-and-relaunch (Continue restores them). Can't-afford
    and already-owned buys are politely refused.
  Your save format was bumped (meta v3 -> v4) to store owned purchases; older saves load
  and migrate forward automatically.


DEBUG / CONFIG MENU — now on P, now tabbed
------------------------------------------
* CHANGED — The config/debug menu is off the first screen. Open and close it any time
  with the P key (in the menu, the hub, or mid-dive — in a dive it pauses the game while
  open). It is now organized into tabs by theme — Hazards / Level Generation / Vision /
  Timer & Quota / Exposure & Return / Throw & Camera / Meta — instead of one long scroll.
  The vision/fog controls have their own Vision tab now. The web "Export telemetry"
  button moved onto the Meta tab. All 89 tunable knobs are still there; "Reset" still
  returns the all-off baseline (byte-identical to M1.0–M1.5) as a control.


NOT YET IN THIS BUILD
---------------------
* Shop upgrade effects are stubbed for now — buying proves the spend-and-own loop, but
  the in-dive effects of the upgrades are not wired up yet (that's a later milestone).
* The Settings menu is a placeholder ("coming soon").
* The dive itself is unchanged from M1.5 (same throwing, room-bound pursuer, hazards,
  quota, and timer) — M1.6 is the surface around it, not new dive mechanics.

M1.5 — "Agency & Legibility"   (changes since M1.4)
---------------------------------------------------

This version is the M1.5 re-gate build. Headline goal: give the player AGENCY against
danger (you can fight back now), make the pursuing hazard a COMPREHENSIBLE, place-bound
threat, and fix two LEGIBILITY nags. Everything below ships ON in the default play preset;
the all-off baseline is unchanged (byte-for-byte identical to M1.0–M1.4) so every new
system can be toggled/tuned from the in-game config menu.


AGENCY
------
* NEW — Throwing mechanic. You can now fight back. The selector cycles a highlighted
  inventory item (scroll wheel, or Q/E; wraps around your bag and re-validates if the bag
  changes). You AIM with the mouse (the player turns to point at the cursor) and THROW with
  a left-click — the item flies toward where you point. A hit on a hazard (the pursuer, or a
  ping-pong bouncer) KILLS it and consumes the item; a MISS (it hits a wall or flies out of
  range) drops the item back into the world as a grabbable pickup, so a thrown item is never
  simply lost. On a controller it's twin-stick: the right stick aims and turns the player,
  the right trigger throws, the bumpers cycle the highlight.


THREAT LEGIBILITY
-----------------
* NEW — Room-bound slow-patrol pursuer. The pursuing hazard is no longer an incomprehensible
  always-on chaser. It now PATROLS its spawn room at a slow pace and chases you ONLY while
  you are inside that room — step out of the room and it goes back to patrolling. The threat
  is place-bound and answerable: leave the room, or turn and throw an item to kill it.
  (Patrol speed is ≈ half of its chase speed, so out-manoeuvring it inside one room is
  plausible.) This was the "spawn-room-only" mode noted as queued in the M1.4 build.


LEGIBILITY FIXES
----------------
* Money readout moved. Your run-haul money now sits BELOW the dive timer (top-right,
  right-aligned) instead of hiding behind the bottom-right inventory grid.
* Grab prompt is context-correct. The grab/interaction prompt now appears ONLY when a
  grabbable interactable is actually in focus/range, instead of being on screen the whole
  time.


CONTROLS (reworked)
-------------------
Mouse + keyboard:
* Move — WASD / arrow keys.
* Aim — the mouse; the player turns to point at the cursor.
* Throw — left-click (throws the highlighted item toward the cursor). Space still works.
* Cycle highlight — scroll wheel (Q / E still work).
* F — grab / interact AND extract / descend (a single contextual action button).

Controller (NEW — twin-stick):
* Move — left stick / d-pad.
* Aim — right stick (the player turns to point that way).
* Throw — right trigger (RT).
* Cycle highlight — left / right bumpers (LB / RB).
* A — grab / interact / extract.  B — extract.  Start — pause.

(The dive timer and money read top-right.)


NOT YET IN THIS BUILD
---------------------
* A thrown item only kills the pursuer and the ping-pong bouncer; the anchored bomb and
  rotating-spike hazards are not throw-killable (out of scope for this build).
* Audio for the throw / kill / near-end-timer cues is still visual-only (audio is M2-gated).


NOTES FOR PLAYTESTING
---------------------
* All new systems are data-driven knobs reachable from the in-game config menu (89 total).
  "Reset" returns the all-off baseline (byte-identical to M1.0–M1.4) as a control.
* On the web build, use the in-game "Export telemetry" button; on desktop the log is at
  user://telemetry/run_log.jsonl.

M1.4 — "Stakes, Variety & Legibility"   (changes since M1.3)
------------------------------------------------------------

This version is the M1.4 re-gate build. Headline goal: give the dive real STAKES,
more DANGER VARIETY, and better LEGIBILITY. Everything below ships ON in the default
play preset; the all-off baseline is unchanged (byte-for-byte identical to M1.3) so
every new system can be toggled/tuned from the in-game config menu.


STAKES
------
* NEW — Quota + roguelite wipe (K2). Each run now has a money quota. It is checked at
  EVERY run end (extract, death, or timeout) against your cumulative money. Run 1's bar
  is $50 and it rises by $50 each time you meet it. MISS the quota and your meta-progress
  is fully WIPED (a 9-field reset) — back to a fresh start. Save format bumped meta v2 -> v3
  with a migration (old saves load).
* NEW — Configurable dive timer + near-end warning (K4). The dive now runs on a real,
  legible clock (default 600s / 10 min) with a near-end visual warning cue on the HUD at
  60s left. (Timeout is a proper run-end cause alongside extract/death.)


DANGER VARIETY — three new greybox hazard types
-----------------------------------------------
* NEW — Ping-pong bouncer (K5a). Travels in a straight line at constant speed, bounces
  off room walls, and kills on contact. Amber tell. Stays confined to its room.
* NEW — Proximity bomb (K5b). Arms when you get close (no defuse), pulses ~2s, then
  detonates in a blast radius. Idle / amber / orange-red tells as it escalates.
* NEW — Anchored rotating spikes (K5c). Three rotating arms with an analytic kill along
  each blade; deterministic phase per instance. Steel/cyan tell.
  All three scale in number with depth, share a combined per-band budget (max 48), and
  each respects a per-room cap. The pursuing hazard from earlier milestones is unchanged.


LEGIBILITY / PRESENTATION
-------------------------
* NEW — Resolution-independent camera (K3). The camera now holds a fixed visible-world
  width instead of a window-resolution-dependent zoom, so "how far can I see" is constant.
  Default visible width is 1000 px (fit-width).
* NEW — Movement jitter fix (K6). Physics interpolation is on and the camera is now a
  level-owned rig (no longer a child of the player), removing per-frame jitter on
  high-refresh displays.
* Depth readout shows "Depth N / max".


SPATIAL
-------
* NEW — Multiple exits (K7). Instead of one fixed gate, a band places several exit gates,
  the count scaling with depth (base 1, +1 every ~10 within-band depths, capped at 7),
  scattered across the level with one always pinned near spawn. Enabled in the default
